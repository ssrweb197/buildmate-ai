How to Measure Staircase Rise, Riser and Tread

Understand the geometry terms used in preliminary staircase layout and why code checks are essential.

Step-by-step method

Measure the vertical floor-to-floor rise and determine a riser count that gives a consistent riser height. Calculate the horizontal run from the number of goings and tread depth. Check the pitch and the available stairwell length, then verify landings, headroom, handrails and access.

Worked example

For a 3,000 mm floor rise divided into 18 equal risers, each riser is about 166.7 mm. The going count and total run depend on the adopted stair layout and applicable code.

Important checks and limitations

A simple geometry calculation cannot establish a compliant or safe stair. Check the local building rules, occupancy, accessibility, headroom, handrail and landing requirements with the responsible designer.

Practical site tip

Keep a written record of the dimensions, units, material source, specification revision, assumptions and date of the estimate. This makes it easier to review differences between an early estimate, supplier quotation and actual site consumption.

Use the applicable project drawings, contract documents and local standards as the authority. Where a decision affects structural safety, ask the responsible qualified professional to verify it.
