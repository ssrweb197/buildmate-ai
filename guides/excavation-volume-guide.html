Excavation Quantity: In-Situ Volume and Swell

Calculate excavation volume and understand why loose excavated soil occupies a different volume from undisturbed ground.

Step-by-step method

Measure the excavation length, width and depth using the specified measurement convention. For regular rectangular pits, multiply the three dimensions. For trenches or irregular excavation, split the shape into measurable sections. Keep in-situ volume separate from loose spoil volume and transport quantities.

Worked example

A rectangular pit 2 m × 1.5 m × 1 m has an in-situ volume of 3 m³. Loose volume requires a soil-specific bulking or swell factor established for the material and method.

Important checks and limitations

A generic swell percentage is only an illustration. Soil type, moisture, excavation method and compaction change loose volume. Use geotechnical or site data for disposal and haulage contracts.

Practical site tip

Keep a written record of the dimensions, units, material source, specification revision, assumptions and date of the estimate. This makes it easier to review differences between an early estimate, supplier quotation and actual site consumption.

Use the applicable project drawings, contract documents and local standards as the authority. Where a decision affects structural safety, ask the responsible qualified professional to verify it.
