Steel Bar Weight Formula: d²/162 Explained

Understand the common nominal reinforcement weight formula, its units and the limits of theoretical bar weights.

Step-by-step method

For a nominal round steel bar, a common quick estimate is mass per metre in kg/m = diameter in mm squared divided by 162. Multiply by total bar length to estimate total mass. For a bar schedule, calculate each bar mark separately and sum the quantities.

Worked example

For a 12 mm bar, theoretical mass per metre is 12²/162 = about 0.889 kg/m. Ten metres is about 8.89 kg before schedule-specific additions.

Important checks and limitations

This formula is a nominal approximation. Actual steel mass, standard tolerances, bends, hooks, laps, cutting lengths and wastage must be handled using approved bar bending schedules and applicable standards.

Practical site tip

Keep a written record of the dimensions, units, material source, specification revision, assumptions and date of the estimate. This makes it easier to review differences between an early estimate, supplier quotation and actual site consumption.

Use the applicable project drawings, contract documents and local standards as the authority. Where a decision affects structural safety, ask the responsible qualified professional to verify it.
