Concrete Curing: Why It Matters and What to Record

Understand the purpose of curing and the importance of following the specified method and duration.

Step-by-step method

Curing limits moisture loss and supports cement hydration and strength development. Select a method suitable for the element and site: ponding, wet coverings, controlled sprinkling, curing compounds or another approved approach. Begin at the appropriate time after finishing and protect the concrete from early drying, rain damage, vibration and temperature extremes.

Worked example

A site curing log can record element, pour date and time, curing start, method, inspection times, weather conditions and the date curing was stopped under the approved specification.

Important checks and limitations

Curing duration and method depend on cement type, concrete, weather, exposure and project specification. Follow the engineer's instructions and applicable standards rather than a universal online number.

Practical site tip

Keep a written record of the dimensions, units, material source, specification revision, assumptions and date of the estimate. This makes it easier to review differences between an early estimate, supplier quotation and actual site consumption.

Use the applicable project drawings, contract documents and local standards as the authority. Where a decision affects structural safety, ask the responsible qualified professional to verify it.
