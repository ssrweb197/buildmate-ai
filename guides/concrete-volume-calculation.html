How to Calculate Concrete Volume

A practical guide to measuring rectangular concrete elements, converting dimensions to metres, calculating wet volume and allowing for ordering waste.

Step-by-step method

Measure length, width and thickness or depth. Convert every dimension to metres, then multiply the three values: volume (m³) = length × width × depth. For an irregular element, divide it into simple shapes, calculate each volume and add them. Deduct large openings or voids where appropriate and follow the measurement rules in the project specification.

Worked example

A slab 5 m long, 4 m wide and 0.125 m thick has a geometric volume of 5 × 4 × 0.125 = 2.50 m³. This is the geometric wet volume before project-specific ordering allowances.

Important checks and limitations

Do not apply one universal wastage percentage to every job. Formwork leakage, pump lines, placement access, uneven subgrade and site practice affect the order quantity. Check drawings and the supplier's batching and delivery terms.

Practical site tip

Keep a written record of the dimensions, units, material source, specification revision, assumptions and date of the estimate. This makes it easier to review differences between an early estimate, supplier quotation and actual site consumption.

Use the applicable project drawings, contract documents and local standards as the authority. Where a decision affects structural safety, ask the responsible qualified professional to verify it.
